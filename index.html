01Summary

TL;DR

Recaptioned image-text corpora are supervision distributions, set by a captioning policy, a captioner and a source corpus. We audit them inside one 64-lexical-unit window with a five-axis profile built on controllable basic units: visual claims counted in the caption and re-asked against the paired image by two judges from different model families. On four cross-corpus pairs our released surface raises supported claims per caption by to under both judges; the release covers image identities across nine source families.

02From the paper

Abstract

Recaptioned image-text corpora are now standard for text-to-image (T2I) training, with VLM captioners replacing sparse alt-text by dense descriptions. Yet a recaptioned corpus is not only a collection of captions: it is a supervision distribution induced by a documented captioning policy (π), captioner (Vc), and source corpus (C). Length-correlated proxies miss caption-register artifacts and downstream T2I benchmarks entangle the corpus with training choices, so this distribution is hard to audit at corpus scale. We introduce a reusable matched-budget audit framework for recaptioned supervision distributions Dπ,Vc,C: at a fixed B = 64 lexical-unit window it reports a five-axis profile spanning prompt-side coverage, image-conditioned faithfulness, and caption-surface health, with claimed controllable basic units (CBU) as the shared denominator. We instantiate the framework on seven paired comparisons over five public source corpora. Across the four cross-corpus pairs, the released surface raises supported CBU per caption by to under both Qwen and Gemma Judges, and on CC12M the same framework exposes a long-vs-dense frontier that is consistent across both judges and four budgets. We release the audited multi-source recap corpus together with the audit-artifact bundle.

03Method

What the audit measures

The framework is a reusable audit operator over recaptioned supervision distributions. Its target is a documented operating point Dπ,Vc,C: the joint over caption c and source image x induced by captioner Vc under policy π on source corpus C.

The audit asks what supervision signal a text-to-image model would consume, without training or ranking a particular generator. It runs four passes over source-aligned rows and returns a five-axis profile; each axis carries an explicit boundary on what it certifies.

Controllable basic units

A CBU is a caption claim of one of eight semantic visual-claim types. The extractor finds CBUs caption-only and the judge re-asks each one against the paired image, so claimed density, supported yield and unsupported risk share one denominator.

With c≤B the lexical prefix of caption c at budget B:

Claimed density CBU/cap(Dc, B) = 𝔼c∼Dc[ |φ(c≤B)| ] (1)
Lexical efficiency CBU/100 lex(Dc, B) = 100 · 𝔼c[ |φ(c≤B)| ]𝔼c[ min(|c|, B) ] (1)
Risk ρ(Dcx) = 𝔼[ u(c, x) ] / 𝔼[ |φ(c)| ]

Supported CBU per caption counts the claims the judge answers yes for; u(c, x) counts the claims it answers no for. Risk is the share of claimed CBUs labeled unsupported, a judge-conditional proxy rather than a direct count of captioner hallucination.

Two judges, one question schema

The captioner Vc is Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-FP8. The extractor φ and the Qwen Judge share Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-FP8; the Gemma Judge, Gemma-4-31B-IT, comes from an independently trained family and re-asks the same extracted claims, so the judge family varies while the extractor stays fixed. Both answer with deterministic, schema-constrained decoding. Each CBU becomes one of three questions:

Is the visual claim '<TARGET>: <UNIT>' supported by the image?
Is the visual claim '<UNIT>' supported by the image?
Is the rendered text claim '<UNIT>' visibly supported by the image?

04Evidence

Key results

Every comparison fixes the source rows and varies only the captioning surface. Caption-only metrics use paired rows per slice; both judges re-ask the extracted CBUs on sampled rows per surface ( on CC12M).

4.1Cross-corpus

More supported claims and lower risk on every pair, under both judges

On each of the four cross-corpus pairs the released surface (Ours) raises supported CBU per caption and lowers unsupported risk under both the Qwen and the Gemma Judge. The supported-yield gain spans CBU per caption and the risk drop spans across the eight (pair, judge) cells.

Length is not the headline

Ours is longer than each reference except LAION-pop, where the two surfaces are length-matched ( vs. lex) and Ours still wins on yield, risk and pool-mass support.

Not carried by the hardest claim types

Without count and relation claims, the types VLM judges verify least reliably, Ours still raises supported CBU per caption by and lowers risk in all eight (pair, judge) cells.

Prompt pools

Per-pool prompt-mass deltas place Ours above the reference on of the seven prompt pools per pair; Figure 3 shows every pool.

4.2CC12M case study

A long-vs-dense frontier, stable across two judges and four budgets

CC12M carries three released comparison surfaces. PixelProse hard-codes openers and descriptive-narrator personas; CC12M-LLaVA-NeXT is a long-form release without explicit opener hard-coding; CC12M-Qwen3-VL† is a short tag-style release ( lex). At B = 64, Ours occupies the high-yield, low-risk corner; PixelProse reads as the highest-risk surface despite long captions (ρ = / under Qwen/Gemma at lex), and CC12M-LLaVA-NeXT sits between.

Length is not density

CC12M-Qwen3-VL† reports the highest CBU per 100 lex ( vs. Ours ) at the short-caption endpoint, but this is the retained-length denominator: claimed CBU per caption is against Ours . Whether its low risk (ρ = /) persists at extended length is not certified here.

Budget sweep

As B grows from 16 to 64, on all aligned images, claimed CBU per caption grows on every long-form surface while CBU per 100 lex decays, as Eq. 1 predicts; Ours leads both readings at every budget. The sweep omits CC12M-Qwen3-VL†, whose -lex captions fit inside every window.

4.3Policy control

Same captioner, different policy: the gap follows the policy

Cross-corpus pairs compare surfaces written by different captioners, so the gain could be read as a property of the captioner family rather than of the policy π. The control holds Vc fixed and swaps only π: a naive surface from the same captioner on the same -image CC12M subset and a parallel DataComp subset, under a single user message that inherits the Recap-DataComp prompt body.

On both source families the naive policy under-yields the released one under each judge; measured risk drops alongside, the expected denominator effect on a lower-claim surface rather than a faithfulness gain. The naive surface is also longer than Ours yet mass-concentrated: frequent 5-token prefixes such as ( captions) and () escape the opener regex but are caught by top-100 prefix mass. After opener stripping, its content-prefix mass () exceeds every released CC12M surface on the same images (). Captioner family alone does not explain the cross-corpus gap.

4.4Human study

Human ratings favor Ours on image support

annotators, blind to surface and machine labels, judged sampled CC12M claims first against the caption window and then against the image: primary judgments on claims balanced over two surface groups and eight claim types, of them ( per cell) labeled twice.

The Qwen and Gemma Judges agree with humans at nearly identical rates. Human raters assign Ours the highest support rate among LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen3-VL-8B and PixelProse; none of its judgments is an explicit no, and uncertainty remains separate from support. The automated and human readings both place Ours at the top of sampled CC12M image support.

05Data

Released captions

The release is a Hugging Face collection with one caption-only dataset per source family: nine families, five of them with the paired audits above and four released without paired audits.

The collection also holds the CC12M and DataComp naive-control surfaces of the policy control. Source images and third-party reference dumps are not redistributed; they are reached through the public source releases and row-level join keys. Caption generation cost H200 GPU-hours.

06Re-run

Reproduce the profile

The audit is corpus-agnostic. A third party supplies caption text, source-row identifiers and, for the image-conditioned axis, paired image access; the released tokenizer, prompt pools, CBU schema, question template, label schema and manifest are reused as they are.

  1. Step 1

    Get the captions

    Pick a source family from the Hugging Face collection. Captions are released caption-only; images come from the source release and join by row keys.

    hf download BootsofLagrangian/cc12m-recap-qwen3p5-35b-a3b --repo-type dataset

  2. Step 2

    Get the audit code

    Clone the code repository. Its README documents the environment and the audit-artifact bundle the profile runs against.

    git clone https://github.com/BootsofLagrangian/audit-recap-t2i

  3. Step 3

    Run the profile

    Text-side axes need captions alone. The image-conditioned axis also needs the paired images and an image-VLM judge served with the published question schema.
